Every player has a separate rating for each game. It goes up when you win and down when you lose, by more or less depending on who you played. Over time, it shows how strong you are at that game.

Starting rating1,000in every game
Ratings5one per game
DrawsNoneevery game has a winner

How it moves

  • Beat someone rated higher than you and you gain more. Beat someone rated lower and you gain less.
  • Lose to someone rated lower and you drop more. Lose to someone rated higher and you drop less.
  • Your opponent's rating moves the other way.
  • Games against people and against computer players both count.
  • Every game in a match-up counts on its own.

New players move faster

A rating starts at 1,000, but a new player might be much stronger or weaker than that. So ratings move further in your first games and settle as you play more:

Games played in that gameHow far one result can move you
Fewer than 5Most
5 to 11A lot
12 to 24More than usual
25 to 39A bit more than usual
40 or moreUsual

Streaks speed it up too: from your third result in a row, wins or losses, your rating moves further, so a strong player reaches their level sooner.

What ratings are used for

  • Computer players: a computer player is set within 25 points of your rating, so it's a fair game.
  • League lineups: boards in a league fixture are paired by rating.
  • Leaderboards: the top players in each game. See Profiles and following.
  • Achievements: reaching set ratings in each game earns badges. See Achievements.

Quick Match pairs people in the order they joined, not by rating, so you may meet someone stronger or weaker.

Fair play

Losing games on purpose to lower your rating, or playing on someone else's account, is against the rules. See Fair play.
