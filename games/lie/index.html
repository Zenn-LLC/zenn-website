Lie on Zentra: rules

Lie is Zentra's own bluffing card game. Play cards face down, claim what they are, and check your opponent when you think they are lying.

On this page
  1. The idea
  2. Playing a turn
  3. Checking
  4. Special cards
  5. Winning
  6. On Zentra

Lie is a bluffing card game made for Zentra, played with Whot cards. Every card goes down face down, and you say what it is. You can tell the truth or not. Your opponent can check you.

Cards6each to start
DeckWhot54 cards
Clock15 mineach

The idea

Lie follows Whot's rules for what can be played: match the top card's shape or number, or play a Whot. The difference is that nobody sees your card. You put it down face down and claim what it is. The claim has to be a legal play on the card below. The card itself can be anything.

Playing a turn

  1. Choose a card from your hand and put it down face down.
  2. Say what it is. The claim must be a card that could legally follow the top claim, as in Whot.
  3. Or go to market instead and draw one card.

Screens from the Zentra app, with sample players and amounts.

Checking

Until the next card goes down, the other player can check the top card. It is turned face up:

  • If it's what was claimed, it stays on top, face up, and the player who checked takes every card under it into their hand.
  • If it was a lie, the liar takes their card back along with every card under it. The table starts clear, so the next player can claim any card at all.

Checking uses your own clock, so take your time, but not too much.

Special cards

Special cards work on what is claimed, not on what the card really is:

ClaimWhat happens
1 Hold OnThe player who claimed it plays again.
8 SuspensionThe next player is skipped, so in a two-player game you play again.
14 General MarketThe other player draws one card, and you play again.
20 WhotYou call a shape for the next claim.
2 and 5Plain cards in Lie. There's no Pick Two or Pick Three.

If a claim is checked and turns out to be a lie, its power is undone: a called shape is cleared, and General Market's card goes back. A true card's power stands.

Screens from the Zentra app, with sample players and amounts.

Winning

Your last card isn't a win until it stands. When you put it down, your opponent either checks it or lets it go:

  • Let go, or checked and true: you win.
  • Checked and a lie: you pick up as above and play on.

You also win if your opponent resigns or runs out of time.

On Zentra

  • Confirm before checking: a setting that asks before you call a lie, so you can't check by mistake.
  • People watching a Lie game see each hand as face-down cards, never the cards themselves.
  • Lie has computer players and game reviews like the other games. A review judges each card you played and each check you made or didn't make.
