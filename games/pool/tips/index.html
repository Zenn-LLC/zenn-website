Pool tips for Zentra

How to win more 8-ball games on Zentra: position play, safeties, planning a run-out and avoiding fouls.

On this page
  1. Think one ball ahead
  2. Plan the whole run
  3. Play safe when you need to
  4. Avoid fouls
  5. Ball in hand
  6. The 8-ball

Potting balls wins shots. Leaving the cue ball in the right place wins games. These habits help you do both.

Think one ball ahead

  • Before each shot, decide where you want the cue ball to stop for the next one. Then choose the shot and power that gets it there.
  • Softer shots are easier to control. Hit only as hard as the shot needs.
  • Avoid leaving the cue ball against a cushion or close behind another ball. It limits your next shot.

Plan the whole run

  • When the table is open after the break, pick the group that's easier to clear, not just the first ball that's easy to pot.
  • Look for trouble early: balls touching each other or stuck on a cushion. Break them up while you still have other shots.
  • Save an easy ball near a pocket as a way to get into position for the 8-ball.

Play safe when you need to

  • If there's no good pot, play a safety: hit one of your balls and leave the cue ball where your opponent has no easy shot.
  • Remember the cushion rule. A safety where nothing goes in and no ball reaches a cushion is a foul and gives ball in hand.

Avoid fouls

  • Ball in hand is a big advantage for your opponent. A missed pot that leaves them nothing is better than a foul.
  • Check the cue ball's path after contact. Shots towards a pocket can follow the object ball in for a scratch.
  • On an open table, don't hit the 8-ball first. It's a foul.

Ball in hand

When your opponent fouls, take your time placing the cue ball. Put it where you can pot a ball and get position on the next, or where you can break up a cluster.

The 8-ball

Before potting your last group ball, think about the 8-ball. Leave yourself a simple straight shot rather than a hard one. Potting the 8 too early loses, and so does scratching while you pot it.
