Skill and fairness

Game reviews on Zentra Arena

Look back at any game you played: every move labelled from brilliant to blunder, your accuracy and coach notes.

On this page
  1. Opening a review
  2. Move labels
  3. What a review shows
  4. Card and dice games
  5. Why it matters

After every game, Zentra Arena reviews it for you. Each move is checked against the best move available, labelled, and summed up as an accuracy score, with short notes on the moments that mattered. Reviews work for all five games.

Opening a review

Tap Game review on the end-of-game screen, or open it later from your finished matches on Home or from Notifications. It's ready shortly after the game ends.

Move labels

LabelMeaning
BrilliantNot the obvious move, and the right one. In chess, often a sacrifice that works.
GreatThe one move that works, where everything else gives ground.
BestThe strongest move available.
Excellent · GoodAlmost as good as the best, or a sound move.
BookA standard opening move. Outside chess, moves with no choice are shown as Book too.
Only moveChess only: the only legal move.
InaccuracyA little loose. A more precise move was there.
MistakeIt handed your opponent the easier game.
MissA chance to take control that you let go.
BlunderA serious error, often costing the game.

What a review shows

  • Accuracy: how close your moves were to the best, as a percentage, for you and your opponent.
  • The better move: for each inaccuracy, mistake or blunder.
  • Winning chances: how your chances moved through the game.
  • Coach notes: short explanations of the key moments, in plain words.

Screens from the Zentra Arena app, with sample players and amounts.

Card and dice games

In Whot, Lie and Ludo, a review judges each choice by the winning chances it left you, given what you could see at the time: your own cards, what had been played, and the dice you rolled. Luck isn't held against you. A good choice that lost to the next card is still a good choice. Lie reviews also judge each check you made or didn't make.

Why it matters

  • The fastest way to improve is to look at your mistakes and blunders and the better moves.
  • Reviews are also part of fair play: real-money games between people are reviewed for play that looks too good to be true.
  • Several achievements come from reviews: your first brilliant move, 90% accuracy, a comeback.
